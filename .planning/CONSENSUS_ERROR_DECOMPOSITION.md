## How to read the tables

`gap = our_mae − source_mae` (negative = we win). `our_bias` /
`source_bias` = mean(projection − actual): positive = over-projects,
negative = under-projects. **Reliable = n ≥ 50** for that slice; smaller
slices are flagged and should not be treated as levers on their own (though a
*pattern repeating across many small-n slices*, as in finding #3, is still
signal).

Archetype is an **approximation**, not a Silver usage-share join: RB
`receiving_share` = season-total receiving_yards / (receiving_yards +
rushing_yards) and QB `rushing_share` = season-total rushing_yards /
(rushing_yards + passing_yards), both from `data/bronze/players/weekly/`,
bucketed into terciles per position-season. This is a coarse season-level
tag, not the leak-free rolling usage feature the model itself uses.

## Findings, ranked by actionability

### 1. Early season (weeks 3-6) is the single biggest, most consistent weak spot — cross-position, cross-source

| Position | Weeks 3-6 gap (n) | Weeks 7-12 gap (n) | Weeks 13-18 gap (n) |
|---|---|---|---|
| RB vs Sleeper | **+0.461** (529) | +0.178 (642) | +0.342 (706) |
| RB vs ESPN | **+0.373** (502) | +0.102 (622) | +0.218 (710) |
| WR vs Sleeper | **+0.350** (810) | +0.101 (1,021) | −0.019 (1,134) |
| WR vs ESPN | **+0.291** (763) | +0.016 (926) | +0.008 (1,063) |
| TE vs Sleeper | **+0.368** (251) | +0.116 (316) | +0.143 (350) |
| TE vs ESPN | **+0.276** (254) | +0.240 (315) | +0.116 (355) |

All reliable (n 250-810), all four skill positions, both sources, same
direction: the gap is worst in weeks 3-6 and generally shrinks as the season
progresses. **Hypothesis**: our rolling-average features have at most 1-3
current-season games of history in weeks 3-6, so projections lean heavily on
thin in-season samples; the consensus sources likely blend in preseason
ADP/depth-chart priors and analyst judgment that adapt to role changes
faster. **Lever**: blend a prior-season (or preseason ranking) prior into the
rolling-average baseline for weeks 3-6, with the weight decaying to zero as
current-season sample size grows — a shrinkage-toward-prior scheme rather
than a pure trailing average this early.

### 2. RB magnitude-band miscalibration: under-project the low end, over-project the high end

| Band | vs Sleeper gap (n) | our_bias | source_bias | vs ESPN gap (n) | our_bias | source_bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| <8 pts | +0.501 (553, reliable) | **−2.58** | −0.42 | +0.297 (571, reliable) | **−2.35** | +0.11 |
| 14+ pts | +0.503 (423, reliable) | **+1.39** | −0.62 | +0.318 (407, reliable) | **+1.18** | +0.68 |
| 8-14 pts | +0.122 (901, reliable) | −0.41 | −0.51 | +0.124 (856, reliable) | −0.50 | +0.23 |

Both tails are reliable, both sources agree on direction: when we project a
back low (<8), the actual outcome runs ~2.3-2.6 pts *higher* than our
number on average, while the source stays roughly unbiased. When we project
a back high (14+), the actual comes in ~1.2-1.4 pts *lower* than our number,
again while the source is closer to neutral. The middle band (8-14, the
bulk of the position) is comparatively well-calibrated. **Hypothesis**: our
low-end RB floor doesn't react fast enough to backup/committee opportunity
upside (an RB2 gets a spike week off an injury/role change we haven't priced
in yet), and our high-end ceiling doesn't discount enough for
workload/injury/game-script variance on presumed bell-cow weeks. **Lever**:
(a) add a short-window snap-share/opportunity-trend feature to catch
backfield role changes faster than the season-long rolling average; (b) add
extra shrinkage/variance-discount at the top RB projection tier.

### 3. QB backup/spot-starter under-projection (ESPN-specific, and a recurring pattern below the reliability floor)

The `<8 pts` QB magnitude band vs ESPN is a **reliable** slice (n=87) with a
large, clean signal: gap **+1.850**, our_bias **−6.11** vs source_bias
**+1.48** — we badly under-project this group (mostly backup/emergency
starters) while ESPN stays roughly balanced. The Sleeper `<8` band shows the
same *direction* of our under-projection (our_bias −5.93) even though the
gap is smaller there because Sleeper over-projects the same group (source_bias
+4.05).

This is reinforced by the top-contributor list, even though **no single
player crosses the n≥50 reliability bar** (backups by definition start few
games): J.Winston, N.Mullens, J.Browning, D.Watson, C.Rush, M.Rudolph,
J.Stidham, M.White, D.Lock, M.Penix, T.Taylor, A.Dalton all show large
positive per-player gaps at n=2-16 games vs ESPN; B.Purdy, S.Darnold,
J.Browning, N.Mullens, T.Taylor, M.White, M.Penix repeat the same pattern vs
Sleeper. **The individual instances are noisy, but the same pattern recurring
across ~15 different backup/spot-start QBs in both source files is a real
systematic effect, not a coincidence.** **Hypothesis**: a backup thrust into
a starting role has thin/no current-team rolling history, so the model likely
falls back to a low generic baseline instead of a role-appropriate one, while
ESPN's projections presumably get a manual analyst adjustment once a new
starter is designated. **Lever**: when the injury report / depth chart flags
a new starting QB, override the rolling-average baseline with a
starter-tier floor (e.g. positional replacement-level rate stats) instead of
projecting off the backup's own sparse history.

### 4. RB gap is concentrated in a small set of role-volatile committee/injury backs

Top-20 contributor lists overlap heavily between the Sleeper and ESPN RB
runs: D.Swift, S.Barkley, Z.Charbonnet, S.Perine, C.Hubbard, I.Pacheco,
J.Taylor, C.McCaffrey, A.Jones, D.Achane, K.Hunt, K.Herbert(RB) each
contribute 15-40 MAE-points to their respective RB gap total at n=14-42
games. These are overwhelmingly backs in committees, coming off injury, or
mid-season role changes (e.g. Charbonnet/Perine Seattle committee, Hubbard
behind/ahead of Panthers RB1 shuffles, Pacheco role volatility). **Hypothesis**
— same root cause as #2: workload/role signals lag a season-long rolling
average. **Lever**: same as #2(a) — a fast-reacting opportunity-share feature
would likely shrink both this list and the magnitude-band miscalibration
simultaneously, since they're two views of the same underlying issue.

### 5. QB rushing-share archetype — real, but ESPN-specific

| Rushing-share tier | vs ESPN gap (n) | vs Sleeper gap (n) |
|---|---|---|
| Low (pocket passer) | **+0.428** (324, reliable) | −0.287 (338, reliable) |
| Mid | +0.159 (482, reliable) | −0.379 (508, reliable) |
| High (mobile QB) | +0.156 (405, reliable) | −0.344 (404, reliable) |

Vs ESPN, our weakness concentrates in traditional pocket passers (Low
rushing-share, gap +0.43) while mobile QBs are close to parity (+0.16) — a
real, reliable, monotonic pattern. Vs Sleeper this axis doesn't explain
anything: we beat Sleeper across all three tiers by similar margins.
**Hypothesis**: our QB edge is concentrated in capturing rushing/dual-threat
upside (matches the known strength); ESPN is comparatively stronger specifically
on drop-back passing production, which our model doesn't differentially
weight for pocket passers. **Lever**: lower priority than #1-4 since it only
explains the ESPN gap, not the (larger) overall QB picture, but worth a
passing-efficiency/matchup feature pass if ESPN parity becomes a specific
goal.

### 6. Inconclusive — RB receiving-share archetype contradicts itself across sources

| Receiving-share tier | vs Sleeper gap (n) | vs ESPN gap (n) |
|---|---|---|
| High (pass-catcher) | **+0.394** (480, reliable) — worst | +0.203 (464, reliable) — best |
| Mid | +0.281 (833, reliable) | +0.204 (832, reliable) |
| Low (pure runner) | +0.313 (564, reliable) | **+0.263** (538, reliable) — worst |

Sleeper says pass-catching backs are our worst group; ESPN says pure runners
are. Both slices are individually reliable (n 460-830) but the two sources
disagree on direction, so **do not treat this as a lever** — it's more likely
that the season-total-yards archetype proxy is too coarse (mixes early- and
late-season role, doesn't capture role *changes* mid-season — see finding
#4) than that receiving role is genuinely the driver. A cleaner test would
join the model's own leak-free rolling usage-share feature instead of this
season-aggregate approximation.

### 7. Context only — RB gap was worst in 2022, not an active lever

| Season | vs Sleeper gap (n) | vs ESPN gap (n) |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | **+0.399** (646) | **+0.360** (576) |
| 2024 | +0.310 (607) | +0.192 (628) |
| 2023 | +0.246 (624) | +0.123 (630) |

Both sources agree 2022 was the worst RB season and the gap has generally
narrowed since, but this is the *same current model* backtested
retroactively across all three seasons (not different model vintages per
season), so it more likely reflects year-over-year differences in data
completeness (e.g. snap-count/route coverage) than something fixable today.
Background context, not a numbered lever.

---

## Bottom line for model levers (priority order)

1. Add a season-progress-aware prior/shrinkage for weeks 3-6, all positions (#1).
2. Give RB projections a fast-reacting opportunity/snap-share-trend signal to
   fix both tails of the magnitude-band miscalibration and the committee-back
   contributor list (#2, #4 — same root cause).
3. Add a starter-designation override for backup QBs thrust into a starting
   role, instead of projecting off their own thin history (#3).
4. Lower priority: pocket-passer-specific QB feature to close the ESPN-only
   gap (#5). Do not act on the RB receiving-share archetype split (#6) without
   a cleaner (non-approximated) usage-share join first.
